# Finding clients

The process for selling these demos, written so it can be followed without
remembering how it was worked out. Covers all four verticals: dentists,
property, travel and products.

Everything here was learned by doing it: roughly twenty prospects screened,
fifteen approached, one agreement (which then went cold, see section 9).

---

## 1. The loop

```
build a list  ->  qualify  ->  contact  ->  follow up once  ->  convert
```

Realistic numbers, from what has actually happened: about half of any list
fails qualification, roughly one in ten contacted replies, and of those
perhaps one in three becomes a conversation worth having. So **ten good
emails is a week's work producing maybe one conversation.** Build lists
accordingly, and never judge a week by whether the inbox moved today.

Two rules that matter more than anything else in this document:

- **Qualify before you write.** A personalised email to the wrong business
  is wasted; the filter is worth more than the wording.
- **Get off email fast.** In India, WhatsApp and phone calls convert where
  email dies. Email is for documents; conversation happens elsewhere.

---

## 2. The universal filter

Spend 60 seconds per prospect, in this order. Two or three yeses means
pitch; one or none means skip.

1. **Are they already spending on marketing?**
   Search the service plus their city and look for them in the **Sponsored**
   results. Check Meta's **Ad Library** (free, public, searchable by name).
   Anyone running ads has a budget and measures results.
2. **Do they measure?**
   Open their page, press Ctrl+U for the source, and Ctrl+F for `gtag`,
   `fbq`, `AW-` or `googletagmanager`. No tracking at all means no budget
   and no way for them to value what you build.
3. **Is there a named decision-maker?**
   An owner's name on the site, findable on LinkedIn. No name usually means
   either an associate-run business with no authority or a corporate chain
   with a procurement process. Both are dead ends for a one-person supplier.
4. **Do they charge properly?**
   Consultations that are free, "affordable" positioning or discount banners
   mean thin margins and price pressure. Published premium prices or named
   premium brands mean the opposite.
5. **Is the thing you'd build their core offer?**
   A dedicated page for the treatment or product you would explain. If it is
   one line in a list of twenty services, nobody will pay to improve it.

**Red flags that override everything:** a chain or group with a marketing
department (your advantage disappears), a company with stalled projects or
unpaid-vendor complaints, and copy obviously lifted from another business's
website.

## 3. Dentists (implant clinics)

**Who:** owner-run implant clinics, one or two locations, where the dentist
is the brand. Specialists are better than general dentists: look for MDS
prosthodontists, periodontists and oral surgeons.

**Where the list comes from:** search "dental implants in [area]" and take
the **Sponsored** results first, since those clinics are paying for patients
right now. Google Maps is a weaker source: of ten Maps-sourced clinics, six
failed the filter. Then check Meta Ad Library for the same names.

**Extra signals specific to this vertical:** dedicated pages for All-on-4,
zygomatic or basal implants (the hardest treatments to picture, and the most
valuable); named implant brands (Nobel Biocare, Straumann, Osstem); published
prices; dental tourism or international patients (they decide entirely from
the website); a consultation fee rather than a free consultation.

**Where contact details live:** many Delhi clinics publish no email at all.
Use the WhatsApp number on the site, or the dentist's LinkedIn. Guessing
`info@` addresses wastes the lead silently when it bounces.

**The hook:** a patient is being asked to spend ₹30,000 to ₹3,00,000 on
something they cannot see, described in paragraphs. For dental tourism
patients, add that they decide before boarding a plane.

**Worked examples from the real list:**

| CDIE, Gurgaon (Dr. Jyoti Singh) | Strong | Implants-only clinic, zygomatic work, transparent pricing, content-led |

| Apollo Dental | Skip | National chain, marketing department |

## 4. Property (biggest budgets)

**Who:** mid-size developers with one to three active projects, where the
promoter is reachable. Not DLF, Godrej, M3M, Signature Global or Adani: all
have marketing teams and agency retainers.

**Where the list comes from, and this is the best source in any vertical:**
the **HARERA Gurugram registered-projects register**
(`haryanarera.gov.in/admincontrol/registered_projects/2`), and UP-RERA for
Noida. Every project must register, so the register lists promoter, sector,
registration date and completion deadline.

How to read it:

- The registration number carries the year (`GGM/1078/810/2026/50` is 2026).
- A completion date years out means a project just starting, which is
  exactly when marketing material is being commissioned.
- **"Lapsed Project" is the avoid-list**: expired registration, usually a
  stalled builder who will not pay vendors.
- Each row's **"View Details of Project (Form A-H)"** opens the registration
  papers, which carry the promoter's address and authorised signatory. That
  is verified decision-maker contact data, better than any website.

**Extra signals:** flats above ₹1.5 crore, renders rather than photos on the
site, a downloadable brochure or floor plan (so CAD exists), and Meta ads
running at launch.

**Check for distress before pitching:** search the company name with
"delay", "complaint" or "NCLT", and look at HARERA's complaints section. A
builder who has not paid his contractor will not pay you.

**The hook:** they are selling flats that do not exist, using static renders
and a PDF floor plan. Your page lets a buyer take the building apart, look at
their own floor, and see what the twelfth floor actually sees. No render
studio sells that, because a render cannot be explored.

**Also pitch:** architects, and real-estate marketing agencies and channel
partners, who build a microsite per launch. One agency relationship means
repeat work with no selling.

**Starter names already found (all mid-size, 2026 registrations):** ROF
Infratech, Dreamways Infra, Fantasy Exim, Lamose Infra, Hemis
Infrastructure, Lakshay Buildtech, CCRK Developers, Emperium
Infrastructure, Regional Construction, JMS Infra Build, Landmark Apartments,
Devasya Developers.

## 5. Travel

**Who:** small-group and luxury tour operators, trekking and expedition
companies, destination management companies. Skip OTAs and anyone selling
₹15,000 weekend packages.

**Where the list comes from:** the **IATO member directory**, trekking
operators and their smaller competitors, and Instagram, which is where this
industry actually lives.

**Extra signals:** day-by-day itinerary pages (the travel demo's route beat
maps onto these exactly), trips above ₹1 lakh, international clients, their
own photography rather than stock, an active Instagram.

**The hook, almost literal:** "Your nine-day Ladakh itinerary is a bulleted
list. Here it is on a globe, with each day where it actually happens."

**Note on effort:** the travel demo is the most expensive to adapt, because
each destination needs coordinates, photos and copy. Price accordingly, and
quote the five-chapter package rather than the single-section one.

## 6. Products (configurator)

Two different buyers.

**D2C brands.** Furniture, lighting, eyewear, watches, audio, luggage,
drinkware. Signals: Shopify (search the source for `cdn.shopify.com`), Meta
ads running, three or more colourways photographed separately, order value
above roughly ₹3,000. Skip anything sold in a tube, sachet or pouch, and
skip apparel catalogues with hundreds of items. One product in many finishes
is the shape you want.

**Manufacturers and exporters.** Machinery, fittings, medical devices, auto
components. These are often the better buyer, because **they already have
CAD files**, which removes the biggest cost and risk. Signals: downloadable
spec sheets or drawings, an export or global-clients page, trade-fair
photos, product variants.

**Where the list comes from:** Meta Ad Library filtered to India, and
Instagram, for D2C. For manufacturers: **IndiaMART** (filter Nature of
Business to Manufacturer or Exporter, never Trading Company), trade
association directories (ACMA, IMTMA, IESA), and exhibitor lists from trade
fairs at Pragati Maidan or India Expo Centre. A company that paid for a
trade-fair stand believes in presentation.

Qualify from the IndiaMART company profile: year established (before ~2015),
turnover in crores, export markets, and whether their product photos look
like renders (meaning a model exists). Then **leave IndiaMART** and find the
director on LinkedIn: contacting through the platform puts you in a queue
with sales calls.

**The hooks:** "You shoot every colourway separately; one model shows them
all, and adding a colour next season costs nothing." For manufacturers: "If
you have the CAD file, buyers can rotate it, look inside, and switch
variants, which is quicker to build than people expect."

## 7. Templates

Personalise the **first line** with something only a reader would know: their
page, their product, their own words. That line is the entire difference
between this and spam.

### Cold email

> Subject: [something specific to them, not "3D websites"]
>
> Dear [name],
>
> [One line about their actual page or product, quoting it if possible.]
>
> I build interactive 3D pages for the web. This one [does the thing], and
> it works on a phone with nothing to install:
>
> [demo link]
>
> [One line on the strongest use for their business specifically.]
>
> It's one page that links from your existing site, so nothing else
> changes. Happy to send a plan and price.
>
> Aman

### WhatsApp (shorter, no greeting paragraph, link early)

> Hello, I'm Aman. I build interactive 3D pages for [industry] websites.
>
> [One line about their page.]
>
> Here's one I built: [link]
>
> Could I send the details to [name]?

### Follow-up, once, five days later

> Just checking this reached you. Happy to mock up your [specific thing] so
> you can see it before deciding anything.

Then stop. One follow-up roughly doubles replies; three make you a nuisance.

### When they reply

Ask the two questions that decide price and effort **before** quoting:

1. Do you have a 3D model or CAD file, or only photos?
2. Is this a new standalone page, or inside your current site?

### Plan and price

State what it is, why that page, the scope as bullets, the timeline in
stages, the price with payment split, and what you need from them. Add one
honest line about what you cannot promise (you cannot promise more
customers; you can promise the thing explains itself better and that they
will see scroll depth and taps in their analytics).

### If they ask why they should trust you with an advance

Do not discount. Reduce their risk instead:

> That's fair, you don't know me yet. I'll build the first section and send
> you a live link. If you don't like it, we stop and you pay nothing. Once
> you're happy with that, half the fee to continue, and the rest when it's
> live.

You can also offer to run it through Fiverr, where the money is held until
they approve. Offering that voluntarily says more than any promise.

## 8. Pricing

Benchmarked against live three.js gigs on Fiverr, which start at $150 to
$300, with experienced sellers at $1,500.

| Package | Price | Delivery | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scene | ₹12,000 ($120) | 7 days | One 3D section for an existing page |
| Story | ₹35,000 ($350) | 12 days | A full one-page scroll story, up to 5 beats |
| Configurator | ₹80,000 ($800) | 18 days | Options a buyer can change live |

Extras: extra section ₹9,000; model sourcing and optimisation ₹6,000; CMS or
product-data connection ₹15,000; rush delivery +50%.

Rules learned the hard way:

- **Never discount under pressure.** Reduce scope instead: fewer beats, one
  revision, no price section. Discounting teaches them to push on everything.
- **Take the advance before building.** Not for the money: it is the moment
  someone becomes a client rather than an enthusiast.
- **Always see the model or CAD file before quoting.** It is the single
  biggest factor in effort.
- **Offer area exclusivity** to local businesses ("I won't build this for
  another implant clinic in Punjabi Bagh for six months"). It costs little
  and is worth real money to them.
- Quote in rupees locally. Expect negotiation below the Fiverr equivalents.
- For a first client in a vertical, trading a discount for **permission to
  show the work** is worth more than the fee.

## 9. What went wrong once, so it does not again

The first agreement (Dr. Sachdeva, September 2026) came from a cold email,
reached "go ahead with this", and then died in silence. The causes, in order:

1. **The reply after his yes gave him homework:** four things to send,
   including chasing his web developer. Tasks get postponed; decisions do not.
2. **It asked for money without saying how to pay.** No UPI ID, no bank
   details, no invoice. Even a willing client could not act.
3. **Nothing had been built yet.** He had agreed enthusiastically and then
   waited, and enthusiasm cools within days.
4. **Everything stayed in email.** No call was made for two weeks.

The corrected sequence: **reply the same day with one question, start
building immediately, send a live link within 48 hours, call within the
first week, and attach an invoice as a PDF with UPI and bank details.**

## 10. Weekly rhythm

- **Monday:** build or extend one list, ten qualified prospects.
- **Tuesday and Wednesday:** send, five a day, each personalised. WhatsApp
  where there is no email.
- **Friday:** one follow-up to everyone contacted the previous week.
- **Any reply:** respond within hours, ask the two questions, offer a call.
- **Every fortnight:** check Fiverr impressions and clicks (do not edit the
  gigs more often than that; editing resets their standing in search).

Track in a spreadsheet: business, contact name, channel, date sent, date
followed up, reply, outcome, notes. Five columns beat memory.

## 11. Fiverr, in proportion

Three gigs are live (the broad 3D website one, a product configurator one, a
scroll animation one). They produced spam within minutes of publishing and
nothing else. Expect weeks before impressions build, because the category is
low-volume and new sellers rank low without reviews.

Treat Fiverr as a shopfront that occasionally sells something, and outbound
as the actual business. **The one agreement so far came from a cold email.**

Fiverr housekeeping: report spam rather than ignoring it (ignoring hurts
response rate; messages from accounts Fiverr removes do not), keep demo
links for conversations rather than gig descriptions, and reply fast, since
buyers message several sellers and hire whoever answers first with something
useful.
